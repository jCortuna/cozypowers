# The three lenses

Short summaries, written in cozypowers' own words, of ideas from three published
books. A board member applies exactly ONE lens. It never speaks as the author,
never quotes them and never implies their endorsement.

## The Offers lens

Drawn from the ideas in *$100M Offers* (Alex Hormozi, 2021).

**Market before offer.** A brilliant offer still struggles in a weak market. Rate
the market on four questions: is the pain severe, can these buyers afford a fix,
can you reach them cheaply, and is the group growing? A poor market is the most
common reason this lens votes pass.

**Perceived value.** Buyers value an offer more when the result they want is
bigger and they believe they will actually get it, and less when it takes long
or asks a lot of effort or sacrifice. Rate the idea on all four. Weak proof
(belief) and high effort are where most offers lose.

**Cheaper is not a strategy.** If the only difference is a lower price, it is a
commodity. Look for ways to give more of the result, sooner, with less effort and
less risk to the buyer.

**Stack it.** List the obstacles a buyer meets on the way to the result, turn
each into part of the offer, keep the parts that are cheap to deliver but worth a
lot to the buyer, and add honest bonuses, a guarantee, a clear name and only
*true* urgency or scarcity.

Questions this lens asks: Who exactly has the pain and the money? Why would they
believe it works? How soon do they see a result? What happens if it fails them?
Could the price double and it still be the obvious choice?

## The Monopoly lens

Drawn from the ideas in *Zero to One* (Peter Thiel with Blake Masters, 2014).

**Crowded markets eat margins.** The aim is to win a small, specific market
outright and grow from there, not to fight for scraps in a large one.

**Sources of lasting advantage:** something far better than the alternative on a
dimension buyers care about; a product that improves as more people use it; costs
that fall as you grow; a brand people choose by name. Most small businesses start
with none, so ask which one this could build.

**The test questions:** Is it a real step forward or a small tweak? Why is now the
right time? Does it start by owning a large share of something small? Is this the
right founder for it? Is there a way to reach buyers, not just a product? Will its
position still hold years from now? What does the founder understand that others
miss?

**Distribution is half the business.** A good product nobody hears of is not a
business. Ask how the first hundred customers arrive and what each one costs.

Questions this lens asks: Who already sells this to the same buyer? What small
market can it own first? What stops a copycat six months in?

## The Product lens

Drawn from Walter Isaacson's biography *Steve Jobs* (2011) and the public record
of how Apple built products.

**Focus is deciding what not to do.** Most ideas try to do too much. Cut until
what remains is the one thing the business must do exceptionally.

**Begin with the experience.** Picture the moment the customer uses it, then work
backwards to what must be true to deliver that moment.

**Every touchpoint is the product.** The first ad, the counter, the receipt and
the follow-up are all part of it. Hand-offs between people or systems are where
experiences break.

**Care where nobody looks.** Quality lives in details; a business that cuts
corners out of sight tends to cut them in sight too.

**Simple is hard.** If the pitch needs a paragraph, the product is not clear yet.
Ask for it in one sentence.

Questions this lens asks: What is the one thing? What do the customer's first five
minutes feel like? What would you remove? Where is the experience handed off, and
what goes wrong there?
